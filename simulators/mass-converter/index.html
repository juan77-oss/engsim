About the Mass & Weight Converter

Mass and weight units split sharply between the metric and imperial worlds — a US recipe in ounces, a shipping label in kilograms, a body weight in stones, a lab sample in milligrams. This tool lets you enter a value once and see it converted to every common mass unit at the same time, from milligrams up to metric tons, without hunting for a specific from/to pair.

How to Use This Tool

  1. Enter the mass value you want to convert.
  2. Select the unit that value is currently in.
  3. The table updates automatically, showing the equivalent value in every other unit.

Conversion Formula

Every mass unit converts through the kilogram (kg), the SI base unit of mass. To convert from unit A to unit B:

value_B = value_A × factor_A→kg ÷ factor_B→kg

All mass units are linear multiples of the kilogram, so every conversion here is straightforward multiplication or division — mass has no offset scales the way temperature does.

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Kilograms to Pounds

A 70 kg person: 70 ÷ 0.45359237 kg/lb ≈ 154.32 lb.

Example 2 — Ounces to Grams

An 8 oz steak: 8 × 28.349523125 g/oz ≈ 226.8 g.

Example 3 — Pounds to Stones

A body weight of 168 lb: 168 ÷ 14 lb/st = 12 stone exactly, since 1 stone is defined as 14 lb.

Example 4 — Metric Tons vs US/UK Tons

A shipment listed as 10 metric tons: 10,000 kg ÷ 907.18474 kg/US ton ≈ 11.02 US tons, or ÷ 1,016.0469088 kg/UK ton ≈ 9.84 UK tons — three different "tons" for the same 10,000 kg.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many pounds are in a kilogram?
1 kg ≈ 2.20462 lb. Conversely, 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg exactly.
What's the difference between a US ton and a UK ton?
A US (short) ton is 2,000 lb = 907.18474 kg. A UK (long) ton is 2,240 lb = 1,016.0469088 kg. A metric ton (tonne) is simply 1,000 kg and matches neither exactly.
How many pounds are in a stone?
Exactly 14 lb. The stone is still used for body weight in the UK and Ireland — "12 stone" equals 168 lb or about 76.2 kg.
What is a grain, and where is it used?
A grain is a small imperial mass unit (1/7,000 lb, about 64.8 mg), historically based on the mass of a grain of barley. It's still used today for bullet and gunpowder weights, and for medication dosing in some pharmacological contexts.
What is an atomic mass unit used for?
The atomic mass unit (u), also called the dalton (Da), measures the mass of atoms and molecules — about 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg. It's defined as 1/12 the mass of a carbon-12 atom and is standard in chemistry and molecular biology.
