About This Simulator

Welded joints replaced riveting in most machine frames, brackets and pressure vessels because a weld adds no holes, no fasteners and little weight. The price is that the weld becomes the weakest link in a very small cross-section, so you need a fast, reliable way to find the stress inside it. This simulator does that for any group of fillet welds loaded by an eccentric force (torsion) or by a shear force plus a bending moment.

It uses the standard machine-design method: each weld is treated as a line, the throat of a fillet is 0.707 times the leg, and every external load is assumed to be carried as shear on the throat area. Because this model ignores the normal stress on the throat, it is slightly conservative, and it has been validated against tests for decades. The results are compared with the allowable stresses of the AISC building code for the weld metal and for the base metal next to the weld.

Instead of only returning numbers, the tool draws the weld group, colours every point of the weld by how close it is to its allowable stress, and lets you drag the load to see how the eccentricity changes the critical point. It also generates the AWS weld symbol that goes on the drawing and runs a fatigue check with the Gerber or modified Goodman criterion.

How to Use

  1. Step 1. Pick an example from the first list, or start from scratch. Choose SI or US units: switching converts the numbers you already typed.
  2. Step 2. Choose the type of loading. Torsion is an eccentric in-plane force (a bracket loaded away from the centroid of its welds). Bending is a cantilever welded to a support, with a shear V at a lever arm a.
  3. Step 3. Select the weld pattern and enter its dimensions b, d (or r), then the fillet leg h, the electrode class, the base metal and your design factor.
  4. Step 4. Enter the load. In torsion mode you can drag the blue handle on the diagram and the offsets update by themselves.
  5. Step 5. Read the verdict, the safety factors and the leg size required. Follow the red ring on the diagram to see the critical point.
  6. Step 6. Open Fatigue loading if the load fluctuates, and copy the AWS symbol into your drawing.

The Method Behind the Numbers

For a fillet weld of leg size h, the throat width is 0.707 h. Treating each weld as a line of unit width, the properties of the whole group are:

A = 0.707 · h · ΣL J = 0.707 · h · Ju,   I = 0.707 · h · Iu

where Ju and Iu are the polar and second moments of the weld lines about the centroid G of the group, found with the parallel-axis theorem. The stresses are then:

τ′ = F / A    (direct shear) τ″ = M · r / J    (torsion, perpendicular to r) τ″ = M · c / I    (bending) τ = | τ′ + τ″ |    (vector sum)

Because A, J and I are all proportional to h, the stress is exactly proportional to 1/h. That is why the simulator can give the required leg size in one step, with no iteration.

The allowable stresses used for the verification follow the AISC code:

Understanding the Results

τmax and the stress map

The largest combined shear stress on the throat of the weld group. The colour scale is normalised by the allowable shear of the electrode: blue and green are lightly loaded, orange is close to the limit and red means the allowable is exceeded. For a torsion load the maximum always occurs at the point farthest from the centroid.

n — weld metal and base metal

The weld-metal factor compares the shear yield strength of the electrode (0.577 Sy) with the throat stress. The base-metal factor does the same for the plate next to the weld, where the stress acts on the larger leg plane. The weaker of the two controls the joint, and both must be at least your design factor.

Leg h required

The smallest fillet leg that satisfies both the weld metal and the base metal, together with the minimum size set by the thickness of the thicker plate when you provide it. The note under the value says which of the three limits governs.

Fatigue factor nf

Computed from the alternating and mean shear at the worst point of the weld, with the fatigue stress-concentration factor Kfs of the weld type, the Marin factors for a forged surface (welds are assumed to be made over an unfinished surface) and the weaker ultimate strength of electrode and base metal. A value above 1 means infinite life is predicted.

Reading the AWS Weld Symbol

The American Welding Society symbol puts all the welding information on a horizontal reference line. The arrow points to the joint. A fillet weld on the arrow side is drawn below the line and one on the other side above it. The triangle always has its vertical leg on the left, the leg size is written to the left of the triangle and the weld length to its right. A circle at the elbow means weld all around, a flag means field weld, and the tail holds the electrode or process specification. This simulator builds one symbol for each group of equal welds and highlights the welds it refers to when you hover over it.

Worked Example

Eccentric load on a C-shaped weld

A bracket plate is welded to a channel with three 6 mm fillets: two horizontal welds of 56 mm and one vertical weld of 190 mm. The plate carries 25 kN at 110.4 mm from the centroid of the weld group.

The throat area is A = 0.707 · 6 · (2 · 56 + 190) ≈ 1 280 mm², so the direct shear is τ′ = 25 000 / 1 280 ≈ 19.5 MPa. The centroid is at 10.4 mm from the vertical weld, J ≈ 7.07 × 106 mm4 and M = 25 × 110.4 ≈ 2 760 N·m. At the ends of the vertical weld r ≈ 95.6 mm, which gives τ″ ≈ 37.3 MPa. Adding the two vectors gives τmax ≈ 43.9 MPa. Choose Eccentric load on a C-shaped weld in the simulator to see exactly this case, and then drag the load towards the weld to watch the critical stress drop.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is the throat of a fillet weld 0.707 times the leg?
A fillet weld has the shape of a right triangle with two equal legs of size h. The shortest distance from the root to the face is the altitude of that triangle, h · sin 45° = 0.707 h. Failure is assumed to occur on this minimum plane, so all area and moment properties use the throat width instead of the leg.
Does the weld size change the stress in torsion?
Yes, in exactly inverse proportion. Area and moments of the weld group are all proportional to the leg size h, so doubling h halves every stress component. The simulator uses this property to compute the required leg without iterating.
Why are there two safety factors, one for the weld and one for the base metal?
The weld metal and the plate have different strengths and fail on different planes. The electrode is usually the stronger material, so the plate next to the weld often governs. The simulator checks both and reports the one that is closer to its limit.
Which Kfs should I use for fatigue?
Pick the weld type that matches your detail: 1.2 for a reinforced butt weld, 1.5 for the toe of a transverse fillet, 2.7 for the end of parallel fillets and 2.0 for a T-butt weld with sharp corners. Use the custom option if your course or standard gives a different value. The factor is applied to both the alternating and the mean stress.
What does the bending mode assume about the stress direction?
The direct shear V/A acts along the weld, while the bending component M·c/I acts perpendicular to the plane of the weld group. They are combined as perpendicular components, and that is why the diagram shows the bending part with the circle symbols for out-of-plane stress instead of an arrow.
What are the limitations of this simulator?
It models fillet welds as lines in a plane, with a simple shear model on the throat, linear-elastic behaviour and static or constant-amplitude fatigue loading. It does not include residual stresses, weld defects, partial-penetration or groove welds, three-dimensional load combinations or variable-amplitude loading. Use it for learning and preliminary sizing; a real design must follow the governing code and be verified by a qualified engineer.
