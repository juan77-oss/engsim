About the Energy Converter

Energy shows up in wildly different unit systems depending on the field: physicists use joules and electronvolts, electrical engineers and utility bills use kilowatt-hours, chemists and nutrition labels use calories, and US heating bills use therms and BTU. This tool lets you enter a value once and see it converted to every common energy unit at the same time — no need to look up a from/to pair.

How to Use This Tool

  1. Enter the energy value you want to convert.
  2. Select the unit that value is currently in.
  3. The table updates automatically, showing the equivalent value in every other unit.

Conversion Formula

Every energy unit converts through the joule (J), the SI base unit of energy. To convert from unit A to unit B:

value_B = value_A × factor_A→J ÷ factor_B→J

Unlike power units (which include the non-linear dBm scale), every energy unit is a simple linear multiple of the joule — so all conversions here are straightforward multiplication or division.

Worked Examples

Example 1 — kWh to Joules

A household appliance uses 1 kWh of electricity. In joules: 1 kWh × 3,600,000 J/kWh = 3,600,000 J (3.6 MJ).

Example 2 — BTU to kWh

A gas heater rated at 100,000 BTU: 100,000 × 1,055.056 J = 105,505,600 J, then ÷ 3,600,000 J/kWh ≈ 29.3 kWh.

Example 3 — Calories to Joules

A 250 kcal snack: 250 × 4,184 J = 1,046,000 J (about 1.05 MJ) of food energy.

Example 4 — eV to Joules

A photon with 2 eV of energy: 2 × 1.602176634×10⁻¹⁹ J = 3.204 × 10⁻¹⁹ J — a good illustration of why the eV exists as a practical unit at atomic scales.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many joules are in 1 kWh?
1 kWh equals exactly 3,600,000 joules (3.6 MJ), since a kilowatt-hour is 1,000 watts sustained for 3,600 seconds.
What's the difference between a calorie and a Calorie?
A lowercase "calorie" (cal) is the small thermochemical calorie, equal to 4.184 J. A capitalized "Calorie" on nutrition labels is actually a kilocalorie (1,000 cal, or 4,184 J).
How do I convert BTU to kWh?
Multiply the BTU value by 1,055.056 to get joules, then divide by 3,600,000. Equivalently, 1 BTU ≈ 0.000293 kWh, so 1 kWh ≈ 3,412 BTU.
What is a therm, and how does it relate to BTU?
A therm (US) is defined as 100,000 BTU, commonly used for natural gas billing. 1 therm equals about 105.48 MJ or 29.3 kWh.
Why use electronvolts instead of joules?
The joule is far too large a unit for atomic and subatomic energies. The electronvolt (1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J) keeps numbers in physics — like photon or particle energies — in a convenient, human-readable range.
Is an erg the same as a joule?
No. The erg is the CGS (centimetre-gram-second) unit of energy, equal to 10⁻⁷ J. It takes 10,000,000 ergs to make one joule.
