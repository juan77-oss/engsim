About the Fuel Consumption Converter

Fuel economy is reported two fundamentally different ways around the world: as "distance per volume" (miles per gallon in the US and UK, kilometers per liter elsewhere) or as "volume per distance" (liters per 100 km in most of Europe and beyond). These aren't just different units — they're inverses of each other, which is why a bigger mpg number means better economy, but a bigger L/100km number means worse economy. This tool lets you enter a value once and see it converted to every common fuel economy unit at the same time.

How to Use This Tool

  1. Enter the fuel economy value you want to convert.
  2. Select the unit that value is currently in.
  3. The table updates automatically, showing the equivalent value in every other unit.

Values must be positive — a fuel economy of zero or less isn't physically meaningful (it would imply infinite or undefined fuel use).

Conversion Formula

Miles per US gallon, miles per UK gallon and km/L are all "distance per volume" and scale directly against each other, using kilometers per liter (km/L) as the bridge unit:

value_B = value_A × factor_A→(km/L) ÷ factor_B→(km/L)

L/100km measures "volume per distance" — the reciprocal relationship — so converting to or from it is a division, not a multiplication by a fixed factor:

km/L = 100 ÷ (L/100km) L/100km = 100 ÷ (km/L)

This is why L/100km can't just be added to the same factor table as the other three units — it needs its own reciprocal step.

Worked Examples

Example 1 — L/100km to mpg (US)

A car rated at 8 L/100km: km/L = 100 ÷ 8 = 12.5 km/L, ÷ 0.425143707 ≈ 29.4 mpg (US).

Example 2 — mpg (US) to L/100km

A car rated at 30 mpg (US): 30 × 0.425143707 ≈ 12.75 km/L, then 100 ÷ 12.75 ≈ 7.84 L/100km.

Example 3 — mpg (US) vs mpg (UK)

A car advertised as "40 mpg" without specifying which gallon: in US gallons that's 40 × 0.425143707 ≈ 17.0 km/L; in UK gallons, 40 × 0.354006190 ≈ 14.2 km/L — a meaningful difference, which is why the unit needs to be specified.

Example 4 — Why Lower L/100km Is Better

Car A: 5 L/100km (20 km/L). Car B: 10 L/100km (10 km/L). Car A uses half the fuel to go the same distance — for L/100km, lower is always more efficient, the opposite of mpg.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is a higher L/100km worse, but a higher mpg better?
Because they measure opposite things. mpg measures how far you go per unit of fuel — more is better. L/100km measures how much fuel you use per unit of distance — less is better. They're reciprocals of each other, which is why the direction of "better" flips.
How do I convert L/100km to mpg?
First convert to km/L (divide 100 by the L/100km value), then convert km/L to mpg by dividing by 0.425143707 (US) or 0.354006190 (UK). There's no single fixed multiplier from L/100km directly to mpg, because the relationship is a reciprocal, not a scale factor.
Is US mpg the same as UK mpg?
No — they use different gallon sizes. The UK (imperial) gallon is about 20% larger than the US gallon, so the same mpg number represents better real fuel economy in UK gallons than in US gallons.
Why can't I enter 0 or a negative fuel economy value?
A fuel economy of exactly 0 would imply either infinite fuel consumption (0 km/L) or infinite distance per drop of fuel (0 L/100km), depending on which unit you started from — neither is physically meaningful. Negative values have no real-world interpretation either.
Which unit should I use to compare cars fairly?
L/100km (or km/L) tends to make comparisons more intuitive for percentage savings, since it's linear with fuel used — a 10% reduction in L/100km always saves the same 10% of fuel, while a 10% increase in mpg doesn't correspond to a clean 10% fuel saving because of the reciprocal relationship.
